# Two- and three-blank Text Completion frames.
#   @@ cluster1 | cluster2 [| cluster3]
#   signal | sentence using [1] [2] [3] (optionally followed by ed/ing/s) | explanation
# Each blank's choices: a word from its cluster (correct), one from the antonym cluster (trap), one random word of the same part of speech.

@@ taciturn | talkative
contrast | Although she was [1] in large meetings, rarely offering more than a word, she became surprisingly [2] in one-on-one conversations. | "Rarely offering more than a word" fixes blank (i) as silent. "Although … surprisingly" flips it for blank (ii): talkative.

@@ praise-v | scathing
contrast | Critics who once rushed to [1] the director's early films have become increasingly [2], dismissing her recent work as self-indulgent. | "Once rushed to" (then "have become increasingly … dismissing") shows a reversal: blank (i) = praise; blank (ii) = harshly critical.

@@ meticulous | careless
contrast | The first editor was [1], checking every date and name, whereas her successor was so [2] that errors multiplied in every issue. | "Checking every date and name" → very careful. "Whereas … errors multiplied" → careless.

@@ stubborn | compliant
contrast | The negotiator had expected the union to be [1], but its leaders proved surprisingly [2], accepting nearly every proposal. | "Had expected … but … surprisingly … accepting nearly every proposal": (ii) = willing to go along, so (i) = the opposite, stubborn.

@@ obscure | lucid
contrast | Although the philosopher's original treatise is famously [1], her student's commentary is [2] enough for undergraduates to follow. | "Although … famously" vs "enough for undergraduates to follow": hard to understand, then clear.

@@ ephemeral | enduring
contrast | Many assumed the craze would prove [1], fading as quickly as it had arisen, but its influence on fashion has been surprisingly [2]. | "Fading as quickly as it arose" = short-lived; "but … surprisingly" flips to long-lasting.

@@ cautious | reckless
contrast | Investors who were once [1], refusing any risk, have lately become almost [2], pouring money into untested ventures. | "Refusing any risk" = cautious; "pouring money into untested ventures" = reckless.

@@ calm | agitated
contrast | Although the pilot remained [1] throughout the emergency, the passengers grew increasingly [2] as the plane lost altitude. | Contrast between the pilot and the passengers: calm vs. agitated.

@@ humble | boastful
contrast | The scientist was [1] about her own role, crediting her team for the discovery, whereas her director was openly [2], claiming it as his own triumph. | "Crediting her team" = modest; "claiming it as his own triumph" = boastful.

@@ generous | stingy
contrast | The philanthropist was [1] toward strangers, funding hospitals across the country, yet oddly [2] with his own family, refusing even small loans. | "Funding hospitals" = generous; "yet oddly … refusing even small loans" = stingy.

@@ trivial | momentous
contrast | What seemed a [1] disagreement over wording turned out to be [2], determining the outcome of the entire treaty. | "Seemed … turned out to be … determining the outcome": minor, then highly significant.

@@ candid | deceitful
contrast | The memoir appears [1], full of painful confessions, but historians have shown that much of it is [2], designed to hide the author's worst misdeeds. | "Full of painful confessions" = frank; "designed to hide" = deceitful.

@@ cogent | specious
contrast | The prosecutor's case seemed [1] at first, but the defense exposed its central argument as [2], resting on a misread document. | Seemed convincing, "but … exposed … resting on a misread document": superficially plausible but wrong.

@@ naive | cunning
contrast | Though the young clerk appeared [1], believing everything he was told, he was in fact remarkably [2], quietly manipulating his superiors. | Appears trusting; "in fact … manipulating" = crafty.

@@ novel | trite
contrast | The director's early films were praised as [1], unlike anything seen before, but her later ones seem [2], recycling the same tired devices. | "Unlike anything seen before" = original; "recycling the same tired devices" = unoriginal.

@@ ubiquitous | scarce
contrast | Once [1], found in nearly every household, the typewriter is now so [2] that collectors pay high prices for working models. | "Found in nearly every household" = everywhere; "collectors pay high prices" = scarce.

@@ harmful | beneficial
contrast | Although the drug was hailed as [1] when it was introduced, researchers later discovered its [2] side effects on the liver. | Hailed as good, "although … later discovered" harm. (i) beneficial, (ii) harmful.

@@ alleviate | exacerbate
contrast | The new policy, meant to [1] the housing shortage, has instead [2]ed it by encouraging speculation. | "Meant to … has instead": relieve, then worsen.

@@ corroborate | refute
contrast | The historian hoped the new documents would [1] her theory; instead, they [2] it decisively. | "Hoped … instead": confirm vs. disprove. (ii) takes past tense of "refute".

@@ placate | provoke
contrast | Rather than trying to [1] the angry crowd, the official's dismissive remarks seemed calculated to [2] it further. | "Rather than" calming, the remarks "seemed calculated to … further": stir up.

@@ hinder | facilitate
contrast | The regulations, intended to [1] trade between the two countries, have in practice [2]ed it with endless paperwork. | "Intended to … in practice … endless paperwork": make easier vs. obstruct.

@@ dwindle | increase
contrast | While membership in traditional clubs has steadily [1]ed, online communities have [2]ed, attracting millions of new participants. | "While … steadily" vs "attracting millions": decreased vs. grew rapidly.

@@ outdated | modern
contrast | The factory's equipment, once considered [1], is now so [2] that replacement parts are no longer manufactured. | "Once considered … now so … parts no longer manufactured": up to date, then obsolete.

@@ lazy | diligent
contrast | Once [1], the student transformed himself into a [2] scholar, spending every evening in the library. | "Once … transformed himself … every evening in the library": lazy, then hardworking.

@@ joyful | sad
contrast | The mood at the celebration was [1] at first, but it turned [2] when news of the accident arrived. | "At first … but it turned … news of the accident": happy, then gloomy.

@@ genial | irritable
contrast | The host was [1] with his guests all evening, yet with his own staff he was [2], snapping at every small mistake. | "Snapping at every small mistake" = irritable; contrast "yet" → friendly with guests.

@@ ambiguous | explicit
contrast | The first draft of the law was [1], open to several readings, so the committee rewrote it to be entirely [2]. | "Open to several readings" = ambiguous; rewritten to be clear = explicit.

@@ secret | overt
contrast | What began as [1] opposition, expressed only in private meetings, soon became [2] resistance, with protests in the public square. | "Only in private meetings" = secret; "protests in the public square" = open.

@@ uniform | diverse
contrast | The school's student body, once strikingly [1], is now remarkably [2], drawing pupils from over forty countries. | "Once strikingly … now … over forty countries": uniform, then diverse.

@@ orthodox | unconventional
contrast | The composer's early works were [1], following every rule of classical harmony, but her mature style is boldly [2]. | "Following every rule" = conventional; "but … boldly" = unconventional.

@@ sporadic | incessant
contrast | The shelling, which had been [1] in the first weeks of the siege, became [2] by midwinter, continuing day and night. | "Continuing day and night" = unceasing; contrast with the earlier state = occasional.

@@ complex | simple
contrast | The regulations appear [1] to outsiders, but their underlying logic is actually quite [2] once explained. | "Appear … to outsiders, but … once explained": complicated, then simple.

@@ superfluous | indispensable
contrast | The assistant, whom the director had once considered [1], proved [2] during the crisis, handling tasks no one else could. | "Once considered … proved … tasks no one else could": unnecessary, then essential.

@@ pragmatic | quixotic
contrast | Her opponents dismissed the proposal as [1], but she defended it as thoroughly [2], pointing to a detailed budget and timeline. | "Dismissed as" (impractical) vs. "defended … detailed budget and timeline" (practical).

@@ belittle | praise-v
contrast | Though rivals continued to [1] her achievements, calling them luck, the scientific community began to [2] her as the discipline's leading figure. | "Calling them luck" = belittle; "leading figure" = praise.

@@ frugal | prodigal
contrast | The founder was famously [1], reusing envelopes and turning off every light, while his heirs were so [2] that they spent the fortune in a decade. | "Reusing envelopes" = thrifty; "spent the fortune in a decade" = wasteful.

@@ brave | cowardly
contrast | The general's public speeches were [1] and defiant, but his conduct on the battlefield was [2]; he fled at the first sign of danger. | "Defiant" speeches vs. "fled at the first sign of danger".

@@ impassive | passionate
contrast | The actor's face remained [1] throughout the scene, yet his voice was [2], trembling with emotion. | "Face remained … yet his voice … trembling with emotion": emotionless vs. passionate.
