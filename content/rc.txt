### coral | short | Biology
For decades, marine biologists assumed that coral bleaching—the loss of the symbiotic algae that give reef-building corals both their color and most of their food—was invariably fatal to the coral. Recent observations complicate this view. On several Pacific reefs, corals that bleached during unusually warm summers recovered within months, reacquiring algae from the surrounding water. Intriguingly, many of the recovered colonies hosted a different algal species than before, one more tolerant of heat. Some researchers therefore suggest that bleaching, though stressful, may sometimes function as an adaptive mechanism, allowing corals to exchange their partners for ones better suited to a warming ocean. Skeptics counter that such exchanges are rare and that the capacity to recover is quickly exhausted if bleaching events recur every year or two.

### memory | long | Psychology
When people recall an event repeatedly, their memories do not simply grow stronger; they also change. Each act of recollection, psychologists now believe, temporarily renders a memory malleable, so that details suggested during recall—by a questioner, by a photograph, or by the rememberer's own later knowledge—can be incorporated and then stored as though they had been part of the original experience. This process, called reconsolidation, helps explain why eyewitnesses who have been interviewed several times often express more confidence in their accounts even as those accounts become less accurate.

The finding has troubling implications for the legal system, which has traditionally treated a witness's confidence as evidence of reliability. Some researchers therefore recommend that only a witness's first account be given significant weight, since later accounts may be contaminated by the very interviews meant to preserve them. Others caution that first accounts, often given under stress, have limitations of their own, and that no simple rule can substitute for careful attention to how each account was obtained.

### sleep | long | Biology

Sleep may therefore serve several functions at once, and different species may emphasize different ones.

### language-birds | long | Animal behavior
