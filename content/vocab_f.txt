= alleviate | verb | make less severe | exacerbate
alleviate | 1 | make suffering or a problem less severe | The new bridge alleviated traffic on the old road.
mitigate | 1 | make less severe, serious, or painful | Planting trees can mitigate the effects of urban heat.
assuage | 1 | make an unpleasant feeling less intense | Nothing could assuage her grief after the loss.
palliate | 2 | make a disease or its symptoms less severe without removing the cause | The medicine palliates the pain but does not cure the illness.
allay | 1 | diminish or put at rest (fear, suspicion, or worry) | The mayor tried to allay fears about the water supply.
relieve | 3 | cause pain or distress to become less severe | A cold compress relieved the swelling.
> contrast | The government's new policy was meant to ____ the housing shortage, but it only drove prices higher. | "Meant to … but it only drove prices higher" — the intended effect was to lessen the problem.
> continuation | Doctors could do little to cure the disease, but they worked to ____ the patients' suffering with medication and rest. | "Could do little to cure, but … suffering" — to lessen the severity.

= exacerbate | verb | make a problem worse | alleviate
exacerbate | 1 | make a problem, bad situation, or feeling worse | Cutting the budget further would only exacerbate the crisis.
aggravate | 2 | make a problem or injury worse | Running on the injured ankle aggravated the sprain.
compound | 2 | make a problem worse by adding to it | He compounded his error by lying about it.
intensify | 3 | become or make more intense | The storm intensified as it approached the coast.
worsen | 3 | make or become worse | The drought worsened the food shortage.
> contrast | Rather than easing tensions between the two countries, the ambassador's remarks only served to ____ them. | "Rather than easing … only served to" — make worse.
> continuation | The drought had already damaged the harvest, and the sudden frost further ____ed the farmers' losses. | "Already damaged … further" — made worse.

= placate | verb | make less angry; appease | provoke

> cause | Because the shareholders were furious about the losses, the board offered a generous dividend to ____ them. | A generous dividend is meant to make angry shareholders less angry.

> cause | Because the pamphlets were intended to ____ unrest among the workers, the factory owners had them confiscated. | The owners confiscated pamphlets because they aimed to stir up unrest.
